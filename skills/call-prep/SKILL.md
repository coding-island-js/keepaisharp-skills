# Prep me for a call

You have a call coming up. This skill gives you one page to read right before you dial, so you know what to say,
what you need to get, and what to do after. Every fact on the page is checked. Anything not checked is marked.

## Step 1. Gather what is already known

Collect everything about this person and this deal before writing a word:

- Emails and messages with them, or about them (newest first). Note who said what, and when.
- Notes from earlier calls, and any file someone sent (a list, a proposal, a price sheet).
- Their public side: company website, LinkedIn or X profile, recent posts, podcast or talk, job openings.
- The calendar: is a time actually booked? Which time zone are they in?

Write down where each fact came from (the email and its date, or the web page and its link).

## Step 2. Check every fact at the source

- Open the source and confirm it says what you think it says. Quote it exactly when it matters.
- Never round up a number, guess a title, or fill a gap with something that "sounds right".
- If you cannot confirm a fact, keep it only if it is useful, and mark it **UNCONFIRMED**.
- If the user has no answer for something likely to come up (price, terms, dates), say so on the sheet and give
  a safe line instead of a made-up number.

## Step 3. Write the one-page sheet

Short lines, readable on a phone, no wide tables. Use these sections, in this order:

1. **Bottom line.** Why this call, and the one thing you want at the end of it. Two or three lines.
2. **Who and how to reach them.** Name, title, company, time zone, phone or link, best time to call.
3. **What happened so far.** Dated lines: who said what. Quote their exact words where it matters.
4. **Who they are.** Three to five public facts, each with its source.
5. **Opening line.** One or two sentences the user can say in their own words.
6. **The two things you need.** Numbered. Concrete (a name, a date, a yes or no), not "build rapport".
7. **If they pick up / if it goes to voicemail.** A short script for each. Voicemail is under 20 seconds and
   gives one reason to call back.
8. **Supporting facts.** Three to six short lines the user may need: proof stories, numbers, examples.
9. **Questions to ask.** Three at most.
10. **If they ask about price.** The real answer if there is one. If not, a line that promises it by a set time.
11. **Write down during the call.** The blanks to fill: names, dates, numbers, the agreed next step.
12. **After the call.** Who to tell, what to send, and by when. (The `call-follow-up` skill writes that email.)

Put a "Sources" list at the bottom. Mark anything unconfirmed **UNCONFIRMED** right where it appears.

## Step 4. Check it before handing it over

- Read it cold: could the user say every line out loud on the call without notes? If not, rewrite that line.
- Every fact has a source. Every name and number matches its source exactly.
- Nothing private from another client or another call slipped in.

## Example

The user types: "Prep me for my call with Sam Rivera at Acme Trailer Rentals in an hour." (Sam and Acme are made up.)

The agent reads the email thread, finds that Sam wrote "We have three trailers free this month" on Monday, checks
Acme's website for what it rents and where, and writes the sheet. Part of it:

> **Bottom line.** Sam has free trailers. You have customers who need them. Leave with a 15-minute meeting with
> whoever decides, and a day for it.
>
> **Opening.** "Hi Sam, it's Alex. I sent you the customer list this morning after you said you have trailers
> free. Do you have two minutes?"
>
> **The two things you need.** 1. Which trailers are free, and where are they? 2. A meeting this week with the
> person who decides. Get a name and a day.
>
> **If he asks about price.** Not agreed yet. Say: "I'll send you the terms by end of day."
>
> **UNCONFIRMED:** whether Sam decides. His profile says "VP". Ask.
